Aston Parish Church, Birmingham


Parish of Aston and Nechells Birmingham is a Victorian Gothic parish church with Tudor tower and a collection of monuments from the last eight centuries.

Aston Parish Church Memorials

Edward Holte (d. 1592) & Dorothy Holte
Type: Elizabethan Wall Monument
Location: Chancel / North Wall


Classic late-Elizabethan “kneeler” monument to Edward Holte (d. 1592/3) and his wife Dorothy in the north aisle of St Peter and St Paul, Aston. Edward Holte (c.1542–1593) was lord of the manor of Aston, patron of the church, and owner of Duddeston and Nechells. He was the father of Sir Thomas Holte (1st Baronet), the builder of Aston Hall. Dorothy was a daughter of John Ferrers of Tamworth Castle. Edward was buried on 15 February 1592/3; Dorothy was buried in December 1594. The monument is one of the earliest of the major Holte family memorials still in the church. It sits near the older 1514 chest tomb of William Holte and is part of the important sequence of Holte monuments that run through the north aisle.

Insciption:
Here lieth buried Edwarde Holte, Esquier, and Dorothye his wife:
wch Edwarde was Lorde of this Towne and Patron of this Churche or Vicaredge;
He was allso Lorde of Duddeston and Nechels, and of divers other landes within this parishe:
who dyed about Candellmas, beinge about the age of one and fiftie yeares,
in the xxxv yeare of the raigne of Queene Elizabethe,
and in the yeare of our Saviour Christ 1592.

Sir Charles Holte, 3rd Baronet (d. 1722)
Type: Classical Wall Tablet
Location: South Aisle

Large architectural monument with columns, putti/cherubs, and dual Latin tablets; to Sir Charles Holte, 3rd Baronet, d. 1722. A fine classical marble mural tablet erected by his widow, Dame Anne Holte (née Clobery). Sir Charles was a scholar, patron of medicine, and Deputy Lieutenant for Warwickshire.

English translation of the upper plaque

Here lies buried
Charles Holte,
of Aston in the county of Warwick, Baronet,
who was born on the 22nd day of March in the year 1649
and died on the 15th of June 1722.

He was the son of the first marriage of Sir Robert Holte, Baronet, by his mother Jane Brereton, granddaughter of William, Lord Brereton, Baron of Laughlin in the Kingdom of Ireland. In that noble family he spent almost the whole of his early youth, and there he was imbued with those principles of love towards God and towards his country by which it was destined that, in the splendid course of his later life, he should be guided and from which he never departed.

He devoted several years to study at the University of Oxford, where he took the degrees of Master of Arts and Doctor of Medicine. He applied himself especially to this profession because, after the care of eternal salvation, he considered it the greatest service to mankind to relieve the sufferings of the sick through skill in medicine. By supplying medicines also to the poor he restored many to health who would otherwise have succumbed to their diseases, had he not piously come to the aid of both their poverty and their failing strength. Many things which he observed with the greatest accuracy in medicine and natural philosophy would have enriched the public store of knowledge, had not an excess of modesty caused him to consign them all to the flames.

Descended from an ancient stock, when he stood at the head of his family he discharged those public duties which are customarily entrusted to men of his rank in such a manner that, in the performance of them, a most conspicuous and unblemished piety towards the Church of England, together with a pure and refined zeal for public liberty, always shone forth.

During the raging of the Rebellion he managed his private affairs with such prudence that the ancestral estates, which had been seized and laid waste in the heat of the Rebellion and further diminished by the heavy expenses his ancestors had incurred in defence of the royal cause, he left to his heir not only restored to their former condition but greatly increased.

He was a most gentle and indulgent husband, a prudent and most affectionate father, and a shining example of piety and charity. Worthy, finally, to be ranked, and that deservedly on more than one account, among the best of men.

William Holte (d. 1514) & Joanna Holte
Type: Recumbent Alabaster Tomb Chest
Location: North Aisle / Chantry Chapel

1514 William Holte and Joanna chest tomb monument on the north aisle now. An impressive late-medieval tomb chest featuring recumbent alabaster effigies of William Holte (Esq.) and his wife Joanna. William is depicted in plate armour with a lion at his feet, while Joanna wears traditional Tudor costume and headdress. Around the base of the chest are carved figures of angels holding shields displaying the Holte family arms (Azure, two bars or, in chief a cross-crosslet fitchée).

The transcription of the modern commemorative plaque:

THE HOLTE ALMSHOUSE, BUILT IN 1655-56 FOR THE USE OF THE PARISHIONERS OF ASTON, STOOD IN THE VILLAGE TO THE EAST OF THE CHURCH UNTIL THE YEAR 1930 WHEN IT WAS DEMOLISHED AND REBUILT AT ERDINGTON. THE ORIGINAL TABLET RECORDING ITS FOUNDATION WAS THEN REMOVED TO THE NEW HOMES. THIS PLATE PRESERVES IN THE MOTHER CHURCH THE DESIGN AND INSCRIPTION OF THE ORIGINAL TABLET.

“THIS ALMSHOUSE WAS BY THE CHARITY OF SIR THOMAS HOLT LATE OF THIS TOWN & COUNTRY KNIGHT AND BARONET DECEASED PROVIDED FOR AND APPOINTED TO BE BUILT AND WAS IN ORDER THERE TO ACCORDINGLY ERECTED BY HIS GRANDSONE AND HEIRE SR ROBERT HOLT BARONET IN YE YEARE OF OUR LORD GOD 1655 & 56

LAUS DEO”

The plaque records that the original 17th-century foundation tablet from the Holte Almshouses was moved to the rebuilt almshouses in Erdington in 1930, and this modern plate in Aston church preserves both the design and the wording of that original stone.

Abbreviations used on the monuments

The Latin inscriptions on the Holte monuments in Aston Parish Church make frequent use of the conventional abbreviations found on 17th- and 18th-century memorials. The expanded versions given on this page follow the standard scholarly readings. The principal abbreviations that appear on the stone are:

- H.S.E. = Hic Sepultus Est or Hic Situs Est (“Here is buried” / “Here lies”)
- M.S. = Memoriae Sacrum (“Sacred to the memory”)
- q. / quib. = quibus (“by which” / “to whom”)
- Dni = Domini (“of the Lord”)
- Ætat. / Ætatis = Ætatis (“of the age of”)
- Anno Salutis = “in the year of Salvation”
- Eq. / Eques = Eques (“Knight”)
- Baronettus / Bart. = “Baronet”
- fil. = filius / filia (“son” / “daughter”)
- ux. = uxor (“wife”)
- ob. / obiit = obiit (“died”)
- & = et (“and”)

Roman numerals are used for dates (e.g. MDCLIV = 1654). Superscript letters and suspension marks (bars over letters) are also common and have been expanded in the transcriptions for clarity.
