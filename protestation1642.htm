The Protestation of 1642 in Rochdale

This is the earliest list of inhabitants of the parish that has been discovered. It is taken from the original returns preserved in the House of Lords library, which were only discovered in 1870. The Protestation expressed the resolve of those who signed it to maintain the religion established against the Pope.

It was only initially intended that members of both Houses of Parliament would sign when it was drawn up in May 1641, but subsequently it was sent to sheriffs, mayors, and justices of the peace to call together the ministers, constables, churchwardens, and overseers of the poor in every parish so that the inhabitants of the parishes over 18 years of age would take the Protestation dated February 1641–2.

In the parish of Rochdale there were 2,100 persons. This list is transcribed from the original returns documented on pages 542 to 552 of the History of the Parish of Rochdale.

Official (Justice of the Peace)

  • Robte Holte, Esq.: One of the two His Majesty's justices of peace who oversaw the taking of the Protestation for the entire parish.
