Holts in Parliament

The Holt (or Holte) family provided several Members of Parliament, primarily representing Lancashire or the Midlands. The family was deeply rooted in the landed gentry, notably the Holts of Stubley and Castleton, and the Holtes of Aston.Here is a summary of the key individuals recorded in the History of Parliament

The History of Parliament website contains all of the biographical, constituency and introductory survey articles published in The History of Parliament series.

Holt and Holte Members of Parliament

This page brings together Members of Parliament bearing the surnames Holt and Holte, drawn from the records of the English and later British Parliament. Entries are grouped by parliamentary era and include brief notes on family connections, dates, constituencies, and any particularly interesting details. Where a member is connected to gentry or aristocratic lines in Lancashire or to the Holtes of Aston in Warwickshire, this is noted explicitly.

1386–1421 (Richard II → Henry V)

Stephen Holt (d. 1398), MP for Lewes in January 1390.
A Sussex wool merchant active in the Lewes trade routes, Stephen Holt received a royal licence in 1373 to export wool and later served as tax collector for Sussex. His background appears commercial rather than landed.

William Holt I (fl. 1402–1414), MP for Plympton Erle in 1402.
Likely a Devon native, he served as a royal customs controller at Plymouth and nearby Cornish ports and acted as feoffee for local gentry such as Sir William Langford. His parliamentary service was unusual for a non‑gentry figure in Devon.

Thomas Holt (d. early 15th century), MP for Canterbury in 1386.
A lawyer and landholder with property in and around Canterbury, Thomas Holt represents the emergence of professional men in late medieval borough politics. He held lands in Kent and married a woman named Joan. No direct connection is known to the later Lancashire or Aston Holts, but his name marks one of the earliest appearances of the surname in parliamentary records.

Thomas Holt (d. after 1386), MP for Canterbury in 1377, 1378, and 1386.
A trained lawyer established in Canterbury by the 1370s, he practised in the court of common pleas and accumulated property across east Kent. His house was ransacked during the Peasants’ Revolt of 1381, reflecting his prominence in local affairs. His holdings were entirely Kentish, with no links to the Lancashire or Aston branches.

William Holt I (fl. 1402–1414), MP for Plympton Erle in 1402.
Likely a Devon native, he served as a royal customs official at Plymouth and nearby Cornish ports and acted as feoffee for local gentry such as Sir William Langford. His parliamentary service was unusual for a non‑gentry figure in Devon. No connections are known to the Lancashire or Aston Holts.

1509–1558 (Tudor Parliaments)

Thomas Holte (by 1500–1546), MP for Warwick in 1529.
A member of the Middle Temple and of the Holte family of Aston and Duddeston in Warwickshire, Thomas Holte combined legal training with landed interests. He stands at the head of the line that would become the Holte baronets of Aston Hall, one of the most prominent gentry families in the Midlands. Through this line, the Holtes were closely connected to county politics and to wider networks of gentry and aristocracy, including links into Lancashire families by marriage and patronage.

William Holt II (d. 1435/6), MP for Warwickshire in 1414 (Nov.) and 1422.
A younger son of Walter Holt of Aston, he regained the family manor after disputes and became a King’s esquire under Henry IV. His tenure at Aston places him firmly within the early lineage of the Holtes of Aston, the family later famed for Aston Hall and their long-standing county influence.

1558–1603 (Elizabethan)

1604–1629 (Jacobean)

William Holt (c.1571–1637), MP for Clitheroe (1597), Preston (1604–1611), and Lancashire (1621–1626).
A prominent lawyer of Gray’s Inn and second son of Robert Holt of Ashworth, he belonged to the Lancashire Holts of Ashworth, a minor but long‑established gentry family. He purchased the manor of Bolton and became a leading legal figure in the Duchy of Lancaster. His speeches in Parliament, especially his 1607 critique of the Anglo‑Scottish Union, were widely circulated. This is the strongest parliamentary representative of the Lancashire Holts.

1640–1660 (Civil War & Restoration)

1660–1715 (Restoration → early Hanoverian)

Sir John Holt (1642–1710), MP for Bere Alston in 1679.
Son of Francis Holt, a lawyer and recorder, Sir John became one of the most respected Lord Chief Justices in English history (1689–1710). Though not connected to the Lancashire Holts, he belonged to a distinguished merchant‑lawyer family whose estates centred on Redgrave Hall in Suffolk. His parliamentary service was brief but active, and he later played a key role in the constitutional settlement of 1688–89.

Richard Holt (c.1635–1710), MP for Petersfield (1690–1698, 1701, 1702).
Eldest surviving son of John Holt of Portsmouth, he supported the 1688 Revolution and served as militia colonel and deputy‑lieutenant. His speeches in Parliament focused on militia organisation and the East India Company. His estates centred on Nurstead House in Hampshire, with no known links to the Lancashire or Aston Holts.

James Holt (1647–1713), MP for Lancashire in 1685.
A major figure of the Lancashire Holts, James was the son of Robert Holt of Stubley Hall and inherited the vast Castleton estate. Educated at Brasenose, he served on militia and corporation commissions and died “very rich.” His family had held lands in Rochdale parish since the 14th century, making him the principal parliamentary representative of the Stubley–Castleton line.

1715–1820 (Georgian)

Rowland Holt (?1723–1786), MP for Suffolk (1761–1768).
Great‑nephew of Sir John Holt, he inherited Redgrave Hall and travelled widely in Europe, reportedly meeting the Jacobite Pretender in Rome. His voting record was mixed, reflecting shifting Tory and Opposition positions. His estates remained in Suffolk, with no Lancashire or Aston connections.

Sir Charles Holte, 6th Baronet (c.1721–1782), MP for Warwickshire, 1774–1780.
Second son of Sir Clobery Holte, 4th Baronet, of Aston Hall, and Barbara Lister, Charles Holte was educated at Magdalen College, Oxford, and succeeded his brother as 6th Baronet in 1770. The Holtes of Aston had long been among the leading county gentry, and the family represented Warwickshire in Parliament for much of the later 17th and 18th centuries. Through marriage and patronage, the Aston Holtes were connected into wider Midland and, indirectly, Lancashire gentry networks, making Sir Charles part of a broader landscape of regional influence.

Sir Charles Holte, 6th Baronet (c.1721–1782), MP for Warwickshire (1774–1780).
A leading member of the Holtes of Aston Hall, he continued the family’s long tradition of county representation. The Aston Holtes were one of the most prominent gentry houses in the Midlands, with indirect links to Lancashire gentry through marriage networks and shared service in royal administration.

1820–1868 (Reform era)

1868–present

Mary Holt (20th century), Conservative MP for Preston North, 1970–1974.
Mary Holt served as Member of Parliament for Preston North in the early 1970s, one of the few modern MPs bearing the Holt surname. Although not directly tied to the historic Holt families of Gristlehurst, Ashworth, Stubley or Aston, her career illustrates the continued presence of the name in national public life into the late 20th century. Her tenure falls within the era of rapid social and political change following the post‑war settlement and the reforms of the 1960s.

Mary Holt (d. 2021), MP for Preston North (1970–1974).
A Conservative MP during the early 1970s, Mary Holt was one of the few modern parliamentarians bearing the surname. Although not connected to the historic Lancashire or Aston families, her career marks the continuation of the Holt name in national public life.
