History of Rochdale

This section presents a chronological summary of references to the Holt family found in The History of the Parish of Rochdale Henry Fishwick(1889). The history of the Holt families (also recorded as Holte or Hoult) in the Parish of Rochdale is a chronicle of a dominant gentry lineage that split into several powerful branches, including Stubley, Castleton Hall, Grizzlehurst, Ashworth Hall, and Stubbylee. Their trajectory reflects the broader social and economic shifts of Lancashire, moving from medieval feudal landholding to high-status political roles and eventually into the industrial age as woollen manufacturers and merchants.

The Early Plantagenets (1154–1399)

  • 1161 (Henry II): Though the Holts are not yet the primary figures, the foundation of their future domain is established when Sir Henry Saville conveys the wastes of Spotland to the Wolstenholmes for a rental that eventually became “a red rose and a pepper corne”.
  • 1277 (Edward I): The earliest local mention of the family's primary seat occurs when Nicholas de Stobbeley (Stubley) appears as a witness to a charter.
  • 1299 (Edward I): Hugh de Holt and his wife Marie demonstrate significant early landownership by conveying their lands in Whitworth to the Abbot of Whalley.
  • 1307 (Edward II): Elia de Stobblegh acts as a witness to a local charter, confirming the family’s established status in the Hundersfield area.
  • 1318 (Edward II): John del Holt serves as a juror at an Inquisition at Clitheroe.
  • 1336 (Edward III): Henry, son of Thomas of the Holt, is fined at the Rochdale Manor Court.
  • 1345 (Edward III): John del Holt and his son Roger hold a tenement in Honresfield.
  • 1386 (Richard II): John Holt of Chesham dies; his son Robert later marries Margaret, daughter of Richard de Holt.
  • 1393 (Richard II): John de Holte serves as a sponsor for a high‑status baptism.

The Houses of Lancaster and York (1399–1485)

  • 1410 (Henry IV): James del Holt serves as the King’s escheator for Lancashire.
  • 1413 (Henry V): James del Holt stands bail for adherents of the Lollard movement.
  • 1424 (Henry VI): James del Holt receives a charter for key estates.
  • 1434–35 (Henry VI): John Holt holds the tenement of Little Wardle.
  • 1435 (Henry VI): A precept is issued against Robert del Holt for occupying lands without licence.
  • 1475 (Edward IV): Robert Holt of Ashworth Hall appears in legal records.
  • 1476 (Edward IV): Letice Holt marries Henry de Howarth.
  • 1485 (Richard III): Alice Holt marries into the Chadwick family of Healey Hall.

The Tudor Dynasty (1485–1603)

  • 1493 (Henry VII): Thomas Holt dies seised of 140 acres and five messuages.
  • 1494 (Henry VII): Thomas Holt of Stubley dies holding 15 houses and 300 acres.
  • 1496 (Henry VII): Henry Holt dies without male issue, triggering litigation.
  • 1523 (Henry VIII): Adam Holt enfeoffs his lands for his sons.
  • 1523 (Henry VIII): Robert Holt of Stubley assessed at £100.
  • 1532 (Henry VIII): Robert Holt moves freeholders to grant land for Whitworth Chapel.
  • 1541 (Henry VIII): Thomas Holt purchases the Manor of Spotland.
  • 1543 (Henry VIII): Robert Holt resists Sir John Byron’s muster.
  • 1543–44 (Henry VIII): Thomas Holt and his wife attacked during a land riot.
  • 1554 (Mary I): Robert Holt of Stubley (elder) dies.
  • 1556 (Mary I): Robert Holt (nephew) dies seised of 4,000 acres and 80 houses.
  • 1560 (Elizabeth I): Sir Thomas Holt of Grizzlehurst in conflict with the Byrons.
  • 1561 (Elizabeth I): Robert Holt of Stubley dies; his will lists heirlooms and timber.
  • 1573 (Elizabeth I): Francis Holt holds a court baron for Spotland.
  • 1576 (Elizabeth I): Robert Holt of Ashworth Hall dies.
  • 1582 (Elizabeth I): John Holt acquires Balderstone Hall.
  • 1592 (Elizabeth I): Charles Holt of Stubley dies.
  • 1594 (Elizabeth I): Adam Holt of Mosside dies.
  • 1595 (Elizabeth I): Charles Holt of Naden dies.

The Stuart Monarchs (1603–1714)

  • 1605 (James I): Richard Holte of Ashworth Hall is buried.
  • 1606 (James I): Thomas Holt of Stidd confirms a lease of Stubbylee.
  • 1607 (James I): John Holte dies, leaving Balderstone Hall to his son.
  • 1621 (James I): Adam Holt of Lowerplace is buried.
  • 1622 (James I): John Holt of Stubley dies at Castleton Hall.
  • 1626 (Charles I): Manor Survey records Robert Holt at Castleton Hall.
  • 1628 (Charles I): Charles Holt of Balderstone Hall dies.
  • 1639–40 (Charles I): Robert Holt serves as High Sheriff of Lancashire.
  • 1642 (Charles I): Robert Holt discharged from the peace commission.
  • 1646 (Charles I): Robert Holt compounds for his estates.
  • 1667 (Charles II): Thomas Posthumous Holt leases coal mines.
  • 1669 (Charles II): Thomas Posthumous Holt sells Stubbylee.
  • 1673 (Charles II): Robert Holt of Castleton dies.
  • 1676 (Charles II): Thomas Holt dies without issue.
  • 1691 (William III): James Holt serves as JP.
  • 1712 (Queen Anne): James Holt, last male of the Castleton branch, dies.

The Georgian and Victorian Eras (1714–1901)

  • 1718 (George I): Dorothy Holt dies; she founded Holt’s Charity.
  • 1729 (George II): John Holt of Lower Hey dies.
  • 1746 (George II): Valentine Holt executed at Penrith.
  • 1784 (George III): Francis Holt of Lowerplace dies.
  • 1787 (George III): James Holt purchases Stubbylee.
  • 1801 (George III): Richard Holt, attorney, dies.
  • 1808 (George III): John Holt of Crossfield House dies.
  • 1835 (William IV): The Holt in Butterworth is demolished.
  • 1876 (Victoria): Robert Holt purchases Little Clegg.
  • 1889 (Victoria): Richard Orford Holt noted as last of his line.
