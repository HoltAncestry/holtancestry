Lancashire Hearth Tax

The hearth‑tax return of 1666 provides one of the most valuable mid‑17th‑century population records for Lancashire. Levied at two shillings per hearth, it effectively measured both the size and status of households. Rochdale Parish was one of the largest and most economically active in the region, with 1,267 chargeable hearths recorded across its townships. Although the return lists many substantial houses belonging to prominent local families, the Holt households stand out in several areas of the parish. Their distribution reveals a well‑established presence across the Pennine townships, with some branches occupying notably large dwellings.

A comparative record of all known Holt households across the four main hearth‑tax assessments The hearth‑tax was collected multiple times between 1662 and 1674. Lancashire has four key surviving returns:

- 1662 (first levy; patchy survival)
- 1664 (more complete)
- 1666 (best surviving return; the one you’ve been working with)
- 1672 (exemptions and chargeable hearths; very useful for continuity)

Lancashire Context

The Forty-Five: Local Impact in Rochdale
Theme Details
National Crisis The Jacobite army under Charles Edward Stuart advanced into England, creating panic and divided loyalties.
Local Effect Rochdale families faced pressure to choose between Hanoverian loyalty and the Stuart cause.
Manchester Arrival The Young Pretender’s arrival in Manchester electrified the region; Rochdale felt the political shockwave.

Valentine Holt of Stubley

Valentine Holt: Background and Jacobite Commitment
Aspect Information
Family Line Descendant of the Holts of Stubley, long-established gentry of Littleborough and Rochdale.
Character Described by Robertson as restless and unsuited to a quiet rural life.
Decision Travelled to Manchester in 1745 to volunteer for the Manchester Regiment, the only English regiment raised for the Prince.

Carlisle and Death

The Fate of Valentine Holt at Carlisle, 1745–1746
Event Details
Retreat from Derby The Jacobite army withdrew north; a small garrison was left to defend Carlisle.
Siege The Duke of Cumberland’s artillery bombarded Carlisle Castle.
Valentine Holt’s End According to Robertson, Valentine Holt was shot at Carlisle during the siege.
Legacy A tragic figure whose death symbolises the personal cost of the Forty-Five for Lancashire families.

Conclusion: The Holts in the Hearth‑Tax Era The hearth‑tax returns of 1662–1672 reveal a family deeply embedded in the landscape of Lancashire. The Castleton and Wuerdle/Wardle Holts stand out as major landholders, while the Spotland, Butterworth, and Haslingden branches show the breadth of the surname’s reach. Smaller households in Todmorden, Oldham, and West Derby demonstrate that the Holt name was not confined to a single social class or region.
