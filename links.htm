Links and Resources

Internet links for family history, Lancashire research, and Holt‑related archives.

  The Redgrave Park, Suffolk website explores the history and landscape of the park from the 13th to the 20th century. It includes a biographical sketch of Sir John Holt (1642–1710), Lord Chief Justice.

  Salford Hundred Ancestry, Annals and History. Chronology A.D. 48–1886.

  The Holt family history and genealogy page, including information on the origin of the surname Holt and a heraldry section.

  Cronton Origin and History. One of the owners was Thomas Holt of Gristlehurst.

  The High Sheriffs of Lancashire 1129–1947.

  In Lancaster Castle, the Shire Hall displays the Arms of the Sheriffs of Lancashire, three of whom were from the Holt family. To see the page displayed on the original website click here.

  Lancashire Hotpots and the Lancashire Virtual Gateway seek to provide a comprehensive and factual source of information about the historical county of Lancashire.

  The Bacup Lancashire website is dedicated to recording the history of the town of Bacup.

  Catterall history including a description and the history of Little Mitton Hall, Little Mitton and its inhabitants.

  Sudley House – its history, collections and the Holt family. The Blue Funnel line was run by George Holt’s brother, Alfred Holt.

  Archaeological Discoveries in South‑East Lancashire.

General internet sites where information about the Holts can be located:

  FamilySearch provides the International Genealogical Index (IGI) online from the record collections of the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter‑day Saints.

  Origins.net is a major source of key Scottish, Irish and English genealogical data on the web.

  The A2A database contains catalogues of archives submitted from all over England, covering archives from the twelfth to the twentieth centuries and a wide range of subjects.

  Chetham’s Library is the oldest surviving public library in Britain and holds many historical documents. A catalogue of the holdings is available on the internet.

  Manchester and Lancashire Family History Society provides a good resource for local historians and is active in projects to transcribe, index and publish genealogically important material.

  Lancashire Parish Register Society compiles records for the parishes of Lancashire and preserves the parish registers of pre‑1837 Lancashire.

  Society of Genealogists for all those interested in family history research. The society has a library and regular lectures.

  The Public Records Office (The National Archives) is the national archive of England, Wales and the United Kingdom. It houses and preserves the records of central government and the courts of law, spanning from the 11th century to the present day, including the 1901 census online.

  An index of burials at Rochdale Cemetery. Rochdale Cemetery first opened in 1855.

  Documents Online allows online access to The National Archives’ collections of digitised public records.

  The College of Arms monitors the entitlement of people to arms and is the official repository of coats of arms and pedigrees of English, Welsh, Northern Irish and Commonwealth families and their descendants.

  This part of Genuki contains information related to all of Lancashire.

  Rochdale Library has an extensive local studies section. There is a project to create a virtual Local Studies Library and historical books about the Borough have been scanned to create PDF versions, central to research on the development of the Borough and its people. There are many references to the name Holt. View the books on offer.

  Manchester Central Library has a wide range of materials relating to the history of Manchester.

  The Register Offices in the county of Lancashire hold records of local births, marriages and deaths back to the start of civil registration in 1837.

  Manchester Burial Records Online.

  OnLine Parish Clerks project data and records are presented from the earliest records that can be found up to approximately the end of the 19th century, when there were almost 400 parishes within the pre‑1974 county boundaries of Lancashire.
